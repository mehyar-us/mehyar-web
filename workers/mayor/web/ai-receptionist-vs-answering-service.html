AI receptionist vs. answering service

Both exist to solve the same problem: your phone rings, nobody can answer, and the caller hangs up into silence. Where they differ is what happens in the next sixty seconds — and in the days after the call. This is the honest side-by-side, including where the human service wins.

The difference that matters most

An answering service is a better voicemail with a person attached: the call gets answered, and then the work comes back to you. An AI receptionist is built to finish the job — the booking lands on your calendar, the confirmation goes out, the reminder happens. If your problem is calls you never hear about, both fix it. If your problem is the hour of front-desk work wrapped around every call, that is the part only one of them does.

Common questions

Can an AI receptionist book real appointments?

Yes — that is the main difference from a message-taking service. Connected to your calendar, it can offer real open slots and put the booking on the calendar while the caller is still on the line or in the text thread, following the hours and rules you set.

Is a human answering service warmer?

Often, yes. A good operator handles an upset caller or a complex call with a warmth software does not match. If your callers are frequently distressed or your calls are long consultations, that is the strongest argument for the human service — and it is a real one.

What does each one cost?

Answering services typically bill per minute or per call, so cost follows your volume. AI receptionists are typically a flat subscription — The Mayor is free to start and $99 a month for Pro. Which shape wins depends on how many calls you get and how long they run.
