Missed Call Text Back for Small Business: How It Actually Works

The Mayor October 23, 2026 6 min read

It is 2:40 on a Thursday. You are in the middle of a colour appointment, gloves on, timer running. The shop phone rings, rings again, and stops. You cannot get to it, and by the time your hands are free, that caller is already looking at the next salon on the list. A missed call text back for small business is the simple fix for that moment: the second a call goes unanswered, the caller gets a text from your business number that keeps the conversation alive.

No new app for the customer. No callback list to remember at closing. Just a text that says you saw the miss and gives a clear next step.

Why a Missed Call Rarely Waits for Voicemail

Most owners picture a missed call as a message waiting for them. In practice, a caller with a leaking tap, a toothache, or a free Saturday morning does not wait. They want it handled today, and the next business that answers gets the job. The cost is not only the one appointment — it is the rebooking that never happens. Our breakdown of how much a missed call costs walks through that maths. This article answers the narrower question: what does a text-back do, word for word, and what must be set up before it can fire?

Missed Call Text Back for Small Business, Step by Step

Here is how it works with The Mayor, using only what is live on the site today.

1. You connect your business number. Text-back does not work on a brand-new account until this is done. Once you connect your number through your own provider account, automatic text-backs are sent through Telnyx from that number. A Twilio number connects for voice calls; automatic text-backs are sent through Telnyx. Until then, The Mayor drafts replies you copy into your own messaging app. Setup takes minutes, in the site's words — but it is a real step.

2. A call goes unanswered — and the text fires almost instantly. The wording follows your trade. The site shows the salon template with a made-up business name, labelled as a sample: “Hi, this is Glow Studio. Sorry we missed your call! Want to book an appointment? Reply YES and we will find you a time. Reply STOP to opt out.” Your name and your trade's wording swap in. A plumbing version asks if the job is urgent; a restaurant version asks about booking a table. The caller always gets an opt-out line and a simple YES path back in.

3. The reply starts a real conversation, not a dead end. When the caller replies YES, The Mayor follows up with your open times and works toward a booked appointment on your book. That booking shows in your day — who is coming in, what is still open, who to win back — in the morning briefing you get in plain words, in your language.

4. You see the numbers that matter for your trade. The Mayor tracks the measures your shop runs on — for a salon, rebooking rate, chair use, and average ticket; for a restaurant, covers and no-show rate; for plumbing, response time and how many quotes turn into jobs. A text-back is one link in that loop: missed lead, instant text, recovered booking.

What It Costs — All of It, Not Just the Plan

Owners are right to ask what sits behind the headline price, so here is the whole picture as the site states it.

You can start free, with no credit card to start. The free plan is $0 and includes 100 reply attempts a month — roughly 10 real conversations, figuring about 10 replies per conversation — and 10 microphone minutes a month. Every reply attempt counts, not only replies that land, and microphone minutes count from the moment the mic opens. Pro is $99 a month, with 1,000 reply attempts (roughly 100 real conversations) and 120 microphone minutes. One-time top-ups — $4, $8, and $12 — add attempts and minutes for that period only; they do not roll over.

Calls, texts, and your number are separate. They run through your own provider account, billed directly at its own rates — never on your Mayor bill, never marked up. The site's example on Telnyx's published US rates, checked in October 2026 — a $1.10-a-month number plus a month of answered calls, call-backs, and 150 text-backs — came to about $4.50 billed by Telnyx. Rates change, but the shape is the point: the plan is the plan, and phone usage is a small, separate bill in your own name. Compare plans on the pricing page. Cancelling Pro stops renewal; the plan runs to the end of the period you paid for.

A Worked Example: One Thursday at a Two-Chair Salon

Here is what this looks like as an example — not a customer story, and no result is promised. It uses the site's own sample wording and a made-up shop.

A regular calls at 2:40 to move her colour appointment. Both chairs are full, nobody picks up, and in the old pattern that chair likely sits empty while she books somewhere else. This time the text fires from the shop's own number: sorry we missed your call, want to book an appointment, reply YES, reply STOP to opt out.

She replies YES. The follow-up offers the open times the owner confirmed when she set up her services, hours, and booking rules. She picks Friday at 11. The appointment lands on the book before the owner has seen the missed call, and it is there in the next morning briefing. The owner did nothing in the moment — she was doing the work she was already paid to do. The phone got answered in the only way it could be, at the second it mattered.

Who This Is For — and Who It Is Not For

This fits owner-run shops where the person doing the work also answers the phone: salons and barbershops, restaurants, plumbers out on jobs, dental offices at lunch, groomers, repair shops, and med spas — the trades The Mayor's walkthroughs cover.

It is not for you if your phones are already covered every open hour by a front desk that never misses, or if you do not want customers texting the business at all. And it will not help until setup is done: an unconnected number means drafts you send yourself, not automatic text-backs.

Questions Owners Ask

Does the text-back work before I connect a number?

No — not automatically. Until you connect your number, The Mayor prepares drafts you copy into your own messaging app. Automatic text-backs are sent through Telnyx once the number is connected.

What does the caller actually receive?

A short text from your business number in your trade's wording: an apology for the miss, one clear question — book an appointment, book a table, is this urgent — a reply-YES path, and a reply-STOP opt-out. The site shows the exact salon template as a labelled sample.

Is the free plan enough to try it properly?

For a quiet shop, often yes: free includes 100 reply attempts a month, roughly 10 real conversations, and 10 microphone minutes, with no credit card to start. A busier shop will feel that cap quickly — Pro is $99 a month, with 1,000 reply attempts and 120 microphone minutes.

What else will I be billed for?

Only your own phone provider, directly: number rental, calls, and messages at its own rates, separate from your Mayor plan. The site's worked example put a typical month at about $4.50 on Telnyx's published rates.

Can I stop a customer getting more texts?

Yes. Every sample template carries “Reply STOP to opt out,” and that line is part of the wording customers receive.
