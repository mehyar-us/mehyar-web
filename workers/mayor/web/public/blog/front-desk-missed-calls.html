Missed calls

Your Phone Is Ringing Right Now. Nobody's Answering It.

The Mayor October 20, 2026 5 min read

It's 10:40 on a Tuesday morning. You're halfway through a haircut, or under a sink, or up a ladder with a wrench in one hand and a fitting in the other. The shop phone rings. It rings again. By the fourth ring it's gone to voicemail — and here's the part nobody likes to hear: most callers don't leave one. They hang up and call the next name on the list. That caller had a leaking water heater, a card in hand, and a problem they wanted solved today. You never knew they existed.

This is the quiet leak in almost every small business. Not bad work. Not bad prices. A ringing phone and nobody free to pick it up.

How Much Does a Missed Call Actually Cost?

More than the price of the job you lost — because the leak compounds. Run the honest arithmetic for a typical trade business, the way we do in our full missed-call cost breakdown:

  • A plumbing shop fields, say, 40 calls a week. In the real world, a third of them land while the crew is on a job, driving, or already on the phone — call it 12 missed calls a week.
  • Say half of those callers never try again. Six lost conversations a week.
  • If even two of them would have become paying jobs at a modest ticket, that's real money walking out monthly — our guide's worked example for a plumbing business lands around $1,583 a month, and a two-chair salon around $850 a month, using deliberately plain assumptions you can check line by line.

Your numbers will differ. The shape won't: the calls you miss are the most expensive marketing you never bought.

Why “I'll Call Them Back” Doesn't Fix It

Callbacks lose for three boring reasons. First, speed: a customer with a burst pipe gives the job to whoever answers first, and “first” is measured in minutes. Second, the voicemail itself: plenty of people — especially younger customers — simply will not leave one. Third, memory: the scribbled number on the truck's dashboard competes with everything else in your day, and it loses.

An answering service patches part of this, and we say so fairly in our receptionist vs. answering service comparison. A human takes a message. But a message is where the trail usually ends — nobody texts the customer back in the moment, nobody books them into your actual day, and you still start tomorrow morning returning yesterday's calls.

What The Mayor Does Differently

The Mayor doesn't take messages. It does the job.

The Mayor answers your business line in your business's voice — your services, your prices, your service area, your hours — the way a sharp front-desk hire would after a month of training, except it never sleeps, never takes a lunch break during your rush, and never lets a call ring out. While you're under the sink, it's:

  • Answering the call and handling the whole conversation — questions, quotes within the rules you set, directions, hours.
  • Booking the job into your calendar while the customer is still on the line, instead of promising someone will ring them back.
  • Texting back the misses. Any call that still slips through — a second line ringing at the same moment, a hang-up at ring two — gets an immediate text: sorry we missed you, how can we help? That's the missed-call text-back habit that rescues the callers who never leave voicemail.
  • Telling you what happened. You get the summary like a partner would give it: who called, what they needed, what's booked, what needs your eyes. You run the business; it runs the phone.

Who This Is For — and Who It Isn't

It's for owner-run businesses where the phone is the front door: plumbers, electricians, HVAC, barbershops, salons, clinics, cleaners, contractors — anyone whose next job starts with a ring. The busier and more hands-on you are, the more it pays for itself, because your missed-call leak is bigger.

It isn't for businesses whose calls need a licensed professional's judgment on the spot, or offices with a staffed front desk that already answers on the second ring. If your phone genuinely never goes unanswered, you don't have this problem, and you don't need us.

Questions Owners Ask

Will callers know they're not talking to a person on my staff?

They'll know they're talking to your front desk's agent — and what they actually care about is whether their problem got handled: answered fast, quoted straight, booked properly. The Mayor introduces itself as your business's assistant and never pretends to be you.

What happens with a question it can't answer?

It takes the details, tells the caller exactly when you'll personally get back to them, and flags it to you immediately. Nothing disappears into a voicemail box.

Do I have to change my number?

No. Your existing line forwards to The Mayor; your number, your listings, your trucks stay exactly as they are.

What if two people call at once?

Both get answered. That's rather the point — the 10:40 rush that used to leak two calls now books two jobs.

How long does setup take?

You teach it your business once — services, prices, area, hours, the way you like things said — and it answers from that knowledge. Most owners are live the same day.
